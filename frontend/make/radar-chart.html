When a radar wins

  • The choice is a profile, not a number. Vendor A vs B, candidate X vs Y — "stronger overall" is vague, "stronger here and here" is a shape.
  • You are conceding points on purpose. Showing where your option is weaker buys credibility for where it wins. Procurement respects the honest polygon.
  • Three to six shared dimensions. Enough axes to form a character, few enough to read it.

When it lets you down

  • Exact values matter. Radars trade precision for profile; if "by how much" is the question, use bars.
  • Three-plus contenders. Overlapping polygons become camouflage. Two entities maximum — three only if one is clearly the hero.
  • The scales differ per axis. Dollars next to percentages next to counts corrupts the shape. Normalize first, and say so.

Field notes on radars

  • Same scale on every axis. All 1–10 or all percentages — the polygon is only honest when the axes are.
  • Two shapes, one hero. Your recommendation in full color, the alternative muted — the shapes argue, the colors decide.
  • Name the trade-off in the title. "A is cheaper and safer; B is richer" — the chart proves the sentence, the sentence sells the meeting.
  • Keep it to one claim per page. A radar is already dense; pair it with a single sentence, per field note #2.
