When a pie chart wins

  • One slice dominates. "62% of our pipeline is one segment" lands harder as a single massive wedge than as any bar.
  • Five slices or fewer. At that count, share reads at a glance — which is the only thing a pie is for.
  • The audience is non-technical. Everyone alive knows how a pie works. Zero onboarding cost.

When it lets you down

  • The slices are close. 23% vs 26% vs 25% — the eye cannot compare angles. That is a bar chart's job.
  • You have seven-plus categories. A pie cut into twelve slivers is confetti. Group the tail into "Other".
  • The story is change over time. A row of pies makes the audience flip back and forth between slices. Use a stacked bar and let the eye travel one direction.

Field notes on pies

  • Start the biggest slice at twelve o'clock and run the rest clockwise, descending. An ordered pie reads in two seconds; a shuffled one reads never.
  • Label the slice, not the legend. Name and percentage right on or beside the wedge — legends make eyes shuttle back and forth.
  • A pie argues for one number. If your sentence is "organic carries us", the pie is the proof. If you have three sentences, you need a different chart — or three slides.
  • Explode nothing. The pulled-out slice went out with clip art. Use color for emphasis instead.
