When a line chart wins

  • The story is momentum. Growth, decline, recovery, plateau — slopes read instantly, bar tops do not.
  • You need the inflection point. The month churn flattened, the quarter growth bent. Lines make turning points visible; that is where you put your talk track.
  • Two or three series race. Plan vs actual, this year vs last. A few lines on one axis is the cleanest race in the chart world.

When it lets you down

  • Your categories have no order. A line across "North, South, East, West" implies a journey that does not exist. Use a bar chart.
  • More than four series. Spaghetti. Cut series or split the chart — attention is the budget.
  • Composition is the point. If the parts matter more than the movement, that is an area or stacked bar job.

Field notes on lines

  • A missing month is a gap, not a zero. ChartThat draws absent values as an honest break in the line. A fake zero turns "we don't know" into "we crashed" — and someone will ask about the crash.
  • Mark the moment you changed something. The pricing change, the launch. An annotation beats a footnote nobody reads.
  • Thin line, calm color, loud endpoint. The eye travels the slope and lands on the latest number — make that the one it remembers.
  • Truncating the y-axis is legal here. Unlike bars, lines encode position, not length — but say the range out loud when you present, or the room will assume zero.
