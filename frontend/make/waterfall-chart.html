When a waterfall wins

  • Bridging two numbers. Budget vs actual, last year vs this year, headcount plan vs reality. The walk between them is the story.
  • Attribution is the pitch. "Here is where the gap comes from" pre-sells the solution — each step is a lever your proposal will pull.
  • The room is financial. Controllers and partners grew up on bridges; the shape itself signals rigor.

When it lets you down

  • No anchor numbers. Without a meaningful start and end, floating bars are just confusing bars. Use a bar chart.
  • The steps are not additive. If step B does not mathematically follow step A, the bridge is fiction and a CFO will check.
  • More than eight steps. Split into two bridges or group the small leaks — a staircase of fifteen treads reads as noise.

Field notes on waterfalls

  • Gains one color, losses another, anchors neutral. The convention is older than your audience — do not get creative with it.
  • Label every step with its signed number. "+12" and "−18" carry the argument; unlabeled steps make the room do arithmetic.
  • The loss step is your agenda. Put the churn bar in red, talk about it longest, and let the fix be the next slide. The waterfall assigns blame so your proposal can assign budget.
  • Empty steps stay empty. A step with no data is not a zero — it is skipped, and the bridge stays honest.
