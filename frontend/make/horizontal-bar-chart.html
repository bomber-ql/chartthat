When horizontal wins

  • The names are long. Product names, survey questions, department names — text reads naturally on a horizontal axis, rotated labels read never.
  • It is a leaderboard. Top reps, top SKUs, top pages. The top-to-bottom scan matches how people read rankings everywhere else.
  • You have eight-plus categories. Vertical space is cheaper than horizontal — twelve rows stay readable where twelve columns become a fence.

When it lets you down

  • Time is the axis. Months laid sideways feel wrong because they are wrong — trends belong on lines and vertical bars.
  • Two series per category. Grouped horizontal bars read awkwardly; keep it single-series or go vertical.
  • Composition is the point. Parts of a whole stack better vertically — see the stacked bar.

Field notes on horizontal bars

  • Biggest on top. Descending order, always — a leaderboard that is not sorted is a spreadsheet with delusions.
  • Put the value at the end of the bar. The label continues the bar's sentence; gridlines behind names are noise.
  • Color the row you are discussing. The whole board quiet, your hero in full color — the chart points so you do not have to.
  • Mind the zero. Same rule as vertical bars: length is value, and a cut axis is a lie with good typography.
