When a sunburst wins

  • The whole has a whole inside it. Global → region → country; budget → department → team. One drill-down level, shown in place.
  • "Which part of that segment?" is the follow-up. The outer ring pre-answers the question every exec asks about the inner ring.
  • The room likes data. Technical and analytical audiences read nested rings happily — it signals depth without a word.

When it lets you down

  • The room is conservative. Finance committees and board packs prefer stacked bars — same hierarchy, zero novelty risk.
  • Three-plus levels deep. The outer rings get too thin to read. Two levels is the honest maximum.
  • Precise comparison is the point. Angles within rings are for impression, not arithmetic — exact gaps belong on bars.

Field notes on sunbursts

  • Two levels, full stop. The third ring is where readability goes to die.
  • Inner ring carries the argument. Name the region story first; the outer ring is evidence on demand, not the opening statement.
  • Export check: ring, not rings. The editable PPTX flattens to one level — present the hierarchy live, or animate it as a GIF.
  • Composition still sells reallocation. Same sales logic as every share chart — see field note #1.
