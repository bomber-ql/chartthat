When a combo chart wins

  • The sentence has a "but" in it. Revenue up, margin down. Users up, conversion flat. Two truths in one frame, tension included.
  • Scale and efficiency travel together. Absolute numbers on the left axis, the rate on the right — each honest on its own scale.
  • You are managing expectations. Growth looks great as bars; the line quietly warns what it costs. The room sees both before they ask.

When it lets you down

  • Both series share a unit. Two revenue lines need no second axis — that is a plain line chart.
  • The audience cannot hear you. Dual axes need narration; in a deck people read cold, two unlabeled scales get misread. Label them on the chart.
  • You have three-plus measures. Bars plus three lines is a control panel, not an argument. Split the slides.

Field notes on combos

  • Bars take the main axis, the rate takes the right. Percentages live on the secondary axis — never let a 0.26 share a scale with a 61.
  • Color belongs to the axis, not to taste. Line and its axis label in one accent, bars quiet — the eye learns the mapping in one look.
  • Say the two scales aloud. "Left is millions, right is percent" — five seconds of narration saves five minutes of misreading.
  • The crossover is the headline. When the line dives as the bars climb, your title should say exactly that. Chart and sentence, one claim.
