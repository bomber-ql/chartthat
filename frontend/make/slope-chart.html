When a slope chart wins

  • The story is "who moved". Rankings reshuffled, costs diverged, scores improved — direction per entity is the whole argument.
  • Two honest moments. Before/after a decision, year over year. The in-between months would only dilute the point.
  • You want the crossovers. Lines that swap ranks are visible instantly — the rivalry is drawn, literally.

When it lets you down

  • Three or more periods. Slope charts are a two-time-point instrument; longer histories belong on a line chart.
  • More than six or seven lines. Beyond that the crossings become a tangle. Show the movers that matter, park the rest in a table.
  • The absolute level is the point. Slopes emphasize change; if "how big" matters more than "how much it moved", use bars.

Field notes on slopes

  • Label both ends. Name on the left, name and value on the right — a slope chart without labels is a trivia quiz.
  • Color by verdict, not by entity. Risers one color, fallers another — the chart sorts the room's attention for you.
  • Talk to the crossover. When two lines swap places, that intersection is your headline: "Organic now beats paid."
  • Two points in time, honestly drawn. A missing endpoint is a gap, not a guess — the line simply does not pretend.
