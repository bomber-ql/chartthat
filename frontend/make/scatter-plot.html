When a scatter plot wins

  • The question is "related?" Spend vs revenue, price vs volume, hours vs defects. The cloud of points answers it before any statistic does.
  • The outliers are the agenda. One store beating the trend line, one rep below it — the dots off the pattern are where your next meeting lives.
  • You need to disarm a assumption. "More discounts drive more orders" — plotted, the room sees whether that belief survives contact with the data.

When it lets you down

  • One axis is a category. Region names on the x-axis make dot soup — that is a bar chart.
  • Thousands of unlabeled points. Beyond a few hundred dots you are drawing a texture, not an argument. Aggregate first.
  • The room reads causation into it. Correlation is a suggestion, not proof — say that out loud, because someone will not.

Field notes on scatters

  • Draw the trend line. The cloud plus its line is the argument; the cloud alone is a Rorschach test.
  • Name the two dots that matter. Label the overperformer and the underperformer — those names become your action items.
  • Axes need not start at zero. Position, not length, carries the value here — but keep the grid honest and say the range aloud.
  • One scatter, one question. Two variables per chart, one claim per slide — the three-second rule from field note #2 applies double here.
