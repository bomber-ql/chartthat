When a donut wins

  • You have a headline number. Total budget, dominant share, completion rate — the center turns a chart into a KPI card.
  • The deck is a dashboard. Rings tile gracefully; four donuts in a row read as a control panel, four pies read as a bakery.
  • Same job as a pie, lighter touch. The hollow middle removes visual weight — the shares still land, the slide breathes.

When it lets you down

  • Close calls. 24% vs 26% on a ring is as unreadable as on a pie — that is a bar chart.
  • More than five segments. Thin rings shatter into slivers faster than pies do. Group the tail.
  • Two levels of hierarchy. A ring shows one ring. Nested structure belongs to the sunburst.

Field notes on donuts

  • Put the point in the center. "45% goes to engineering" — ring shows it, center says it, done.
  • Four segments, five at the edge. Past that, reach for bars.
  • One bright segment, the rest quiet. The ring's job is to frame the share you are arguing about.
  • Start at twelve, run clockwise, descending. Same discipline as the pie; the eye has habits, respect them.
