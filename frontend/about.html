I built ChartThat because every other tool made a simple job feel like a project.

The story behind the tool · About

I work in advertising. Most of my Fridays end the same way: a spreadsheet full of numbers, a client pitch on Monday, and a deck full of charts that don't exist yet.

I tried everything. The AI chatbots wanted me to describe my CSV in a prompt, wait a while, then paste a picture of a chart into my slide — a flat image, impossible to restyle, useless the moment a number changed. The plugins wanted installs and accounts. The BI tools wanted a data warehouse. One chart cost ten minutes and a handful of tokens, when what I actually needed was almost embarrassingly simple: a table in, a PowerPoint chart out.

So I built the tool I wanted

Paste text, a CSV, or an Excel sheet. ChartThat reads the shape of your data, suggests the chart types that genuinely fit — thirteen of them, from combos to waterfalls — and exports a native, double-click-to-edit PowerPoint chart. Or an animated GIF, for the slides that need to move.

No sign-up. No chat window. Nothing to install. And your numbers stay yours — they are processed to draw your charts, never stored, and never fed into someone else's conversation.

That one detail is the whole reason this tool exists.
